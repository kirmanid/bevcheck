1. OVERVIEW AND SCOPE
================================================================================

1.1 PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Background-research-only output. No implementation. The goal is to scope the
problem and produce an English specification of what the system ought to do.
Findings are written here as ASCII text, with uncertainties noted inline and
consolidated in section 8.

1.2 METHOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Read the full assignment (treasury-takehome.md).
  2. Fetched TTB public pages with curl (ttb.gov): labeling home, labeling
     resources, health warning statements, Beverage Alcohol Manual (BAM)
     pages, anatomy-of-a-label pages, COLA guidance, COLAs Online customer
     page, and COLAs Online FAQs.
  3. Fetched the authoritative regulatory text from the eCFR API (ecfr.gov) as
     XML, for 27 CFR:
        Part 16 - Alcoholic Beverage Health Warning Statement
        Part 5  - Labeling and Advertising of Distilled Spirits
        Part 4  - Labeling and Advertising of Wine
        Part 7  - Labeling and Advertising of Malt Beverages
  4. Fetched TTB Form 5100.31 (the COLA application) and extracted its field
     list; extracted COLAs Online file-format and status rules from the FAQ.
  5. Extracted plain text from the XML and quoted the operative sections.

A note on the "alcohol-specific federal website": the assignment's hint refers
to the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (TTB), a bureau of the U.S.
Department of the Treasury, which administers the Federal Alcohol Administration
(FAA) Act and the Alcoholic Beverage Labeling Act. Its site is ttb.gov. The
authoritative regulatory text lives on the eCFR (ecfr.gov), which is where the
precise field-by-field rules are found. (There is no separate "Bureau of
Tobacco and Beverages"; TTB is the correct agency.)

1.3 PROBLEM IN ONE PARAGRAPH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TTB's Compliance Division reviews roughly 150,000 certificate-of-label-approval
(COLA) applications per year with 47 agents, largely by eye. Each review means
checking that the text on a photographed label (brand name, class/type, alcohol
content, net contents, name and address, country of origin, and the mandatory
government warning) matches the text stated in the application and satisfies
the regulations. Much of this is routine data-entry verification. The proposed
tool should automate that matching: accept one or many label images plus
application data, extract the label text, compare it against the application
and the regulations, and flag matches/mismatches.

1.4 KEY CONSTRAINTS FROM STAKEHOLDER INTERVIEWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Throughput: ~150,000 applications/year, 47 agents, 5-10 min per simple
    application.
  - Latency (hard): results in about 5 seconds per label. A prior vendor pilot
    at 30-40 seconds was abandoned. Quote: "If we can't get results back in
    about 5 seconds, nobody's going to use it."
  - Usability (hard): "my mother could figure out" - a 73-year-old benchmark.
    Clean, obvious, no hunting for buttons. Half the team is over 50.
  - Batch: peak-season importers dump 200-300 applications at once; today they
    are processed one at a time. Batch upload is a high-value feature.
  - Environment: on Azure (migrated 2019); COLA system is .NET. This is a
    STANDALONE prototype, not COLA integration. Prototype must not store
    sensitive data (PII/document-retention rules deferred).
  - Network: TTB's network blocks outbound traffic to many domains; the prior
    vendor pilot broke because the firewall blocked its ML endpoints. This is
    decisive for architecture (see section 6).
  - Judgment nuance: an agent noted "STONE'S THROW" vs "Stone's Throw" is
    "technically a mismatch" but "obviously the same thing" - matching must be
    case/format tolerant where the regulation allows, exact where it does not.
  - Exactness: the government warning is word-for-word mandatory; all-caps and
    bold "GOVERNMENT WARNING:" required; "Government Warning" in title case was
    rejected. See section 3.

2. LEGAL FRAMEWORK AND REGULATIONS
================================================================================

2.1 LEGAL FRAMEWORK AND AGENCY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three layers of law govern alcohol beverage labels:

  (a) The Federal Alcohol Administration (FAA) Act (27 U.S.C. 201 et seq.).
      Requires TTB to approve all alcohol labels and advertising to (1) prevent
      consumer deception, (2) provide adequate product information, and
      (3) prevent false or misleading statements. This is the source of the
      COLA (Certificate of Label Approval) requirement.

  (b) The Alcoholic Beverage Labeling Act of 1988 (ABLA). Requires a specific
      government health warning statement on ALL alcohol beverage containers
      (0.5% ABV and above). Implemented at 27 CFR Part 16.

  (c) The labeling regulations per commodity:
        27 CFR Part 5  - Distilled Spirits
        27 CFR Part 4  - Wine
        27 CFR Part 7  - Malt Beverages (beer and similar)
      Each part specifies the mandatory label fields, format rulews, and
      standards of identity (class/type designations).

Agency: the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (TTB), U.S. Department of
the Treasury. Label applications are processed via the COLAs Online system and
approved on TTB Form 5100.31.

Numbering note: Parts 5 and 7 were modernized by T.D. TTB-158 / T.D. TTB-176
(effective 2022) and now use renumbered sections (e.g. 5.63, 7.63). Part 4
(wine) still uses legacy numbering (e.g. 4.32). This document uses the CURRENT
numbers as published.

2.2 WHAT THE REVIEW ACTUALLY CHECKS (THE MATCHING PROBLEM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------w
A COLA review compares two things:

  (1) The APPLICATION data - the values the producer asserts on Form 5100.31.
      Note (important, see section 4): the form carries brand name, fanciful
      name, product type, source, formula, varietals, appellation, and
      applicant name/address - but NOT alcohol content, net contents,
      class/type, or the warning.

  (2) The LABEL ARTWORK - a photograph/render of the physical label.

The agent confirms the label's printed values match the application's asserted
values (brand name, name/address), and that the label satisfies the format
rules (legibility, placement, exact warning text, legal ABV format, etc.). The
tool automates exactly this comparison. The regulations below define BOTH the
set of fields that must exist and the format constraints on each.

2.3 MANDATORY LABEL FIELDS, BY BEVERAGE TYPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.3.1 DISTILLED SPIRITS (27 CFR Part 5)
Mandatory information is set out in 27 CFR 5.63, split by placement:

  Same field of vision (single side; for a cylinder, 40% of circumference;
  viewable without turning the container):
    > 5.63(a)(1) Brand name (5.64)
    > 5.63(a)(2) Class, type, or other designation (subpart I)
    > 5.63(a)(3) Alcohol content (5.65)

  Anywhere on the container:
    > 5.63(b)(1) Name and address of bottler/distiller (5.66) or importer
    >            (5.67 or 5.68), as applicable
    > 5.63(b)(2) Net contents (5.70)

  Conditional disclosures (only when the condition applies):
    > 5.63(c)(1) Neutral spirits: % and commodity source
    > 5.63(c)(2) Coloring/treatment with wood
    > 5.63(c)(3) Age statement (when required or used)
    > 5.63(c)(4) State of distillation (certain U.S. whiskies)
    > 5.63(c)(5) FD&C Yellow No. 5
    > 5.63(c)(6) Cochineal extract or carmine
    > 5.63(c)(7) Sulfites (>= 10 ppm): "Contains sulfites" etc.
    > 5.63(c)(8) Aspartame: "PHENYLKETONURICS: CONTAINS PHENYLALANINE."
    >            (all caps, separate and apart)

  Plus the health warning statement (27 CFR Part 16), mandatory on all alcohol
  beverages >= 0.5% ABV. See section 3.

2.3.2 WINE (27 CFR Part 4)
Mandatory information is set out in 27 CFR 4.32:

  Brand label (front):
    > 4.32(a)(1) Brand name (4.33)
    > 4.32(a)(2) Class, type, or other designation (4.34)
    > 4.32(a)(4) On U.S./foreign blends referencing foreign wine: exact % by
    >            volume of foreign wine

  Any label on the container:
    > 4.32(b)(1) Name and address (4.35)
    > 4.32(b)(2) Net contents (4.37)
    > 4.32(b)(3) Alcohol content (4.36)

  Conditional disclosures:
    > 4.32(c)   FD&C Yellow No. 5
    > 4.32(d)   Cochineal extract / carmine
    > 4.32(e)   Sulfites (>= 10 ppm)

  Wine EXCEPTION on alcohol content: 4.36(a) - alcohol content MUST be stated
  only for wines over 14% ABV. For wine at 14% or less, it MAY be omitted if
  the "table" or "light" designation appears. Tolerance: 1.0% (over 14%) or
  1.5% (14% or less), or a range statement (max 2%/3% spread).

2.3.3 MALT BEVERAGES (27 CFR Part 7)
Mandatory information is set out in 27 CFR 7.63:

    > 7.63(a)(1) Brand name (7.64)
    > 7.63(a)(2) Class, type, or other designation (subpart I)
    > 7.63(a)(3) Alcohol content (7.65) - MANDATORY ONLY when the beverage
    >            contains alcohol from added nonbeverage flavors/ingredients
    >            (other than hops extract). Otherwise alcohol content is
    >            OPTIONAL unless required by state law.
    > 7.63(a)(4) Name and address of bottler or importer (7.66/7.67/7.68)
    > 7.63(a)(5) Net contents (7.70)

  Conditional disclosures (7.63(b)): FD&C Yellow No. 5; cochineal/carmine;
  sulfites (>= 10 ppm); aspartame (all caps).

  Plus the health warning statement (Part 16).

2.4 FIELD-BY-FIELD RULES (WHAT "MATCHES" MEANS PER FIELD)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.4.1 BRAND NAME
  - Must appear on the label. If no brand name, the producer/importer name in
    the name-and-address statement is treated as the brand name (5.64(a),
    4.33(a), 7.64(a)).
  - May not be misleading as to age, origin, identity, or other characteristics
    (5.64(b), 4.33(b), 7.64(b)); a misleading name may be qualified with
    "brand".
  - Brand name is the ONE mandatory field NOT required to be in English
    (5.55(a)).
  - Implication for matching: brand-name comparison should be case- and
    punctuation-tolerant (the "STONE'S THROW" vs "Stone's Throw" case from the
    interviews). This is a fuzzy-normalized comparison, not byte equality.

2.4.2 CLASS / TYPE DESIGNATION (standards of identity)
  - Must conform to the standards of identity in subpart I of each part
    (5.63(a)(2); 7.63(a)(2); 4.34 for wine).
  - Examples of class/type: "Kentucky Straight Bourbon Whiskey" (the sample
    label in the assignment), "vodka", "gin", "champagne"/"sparkling wine",
    varietal wine designations, etc.
  - Wine 4.34: class stated in conformity with subpart C unless "table"/"light"
    or "dessert"; varietal/type/appellation designations may substitute in
    defined cases; appellation of origin must appear with the class/type in
    certain situations.
  - Implication: verification against a controlled vocabulary of legal
    designations is possible but large; for a prototype, extract the class/type
    string and compare (normalized) to the application value, with a vocabulary
    check as a stretch goal.

2.4.3 ALCOHOL CONTENT (formats and tolerances)
  Distilled spirits (5.65): MUST be stated as % alcohol by volume.
    Allowed formats:
      "Alcohol ___ percent by volume"
      "___ percent alcohol by volume"
      "Alcohol by volume ___ percent"
    Allowed abbreviations: "alc" for alcohol; "%" for percent; "/" for "by";
    "vol" for volume. Examples that comply:
      "40% alc/vol"; "Alc. 40 percent by vol."; "Alc 40% by vol";
      "40% Alcohol by Volume."
    Proof may additionally appear in the same field of vision. Proof = 2x ABV.
    Tolerance: +/- 0.3 percentage points (5.65(c)).

  Wine (4.36): "Alcohol __ % by volume" or a range; abbreviations alc./vol.
    Tolerance 1.0% (>14%) or 1.5% (<=14%). Content optional <=14% with
    table/light designation.

  Malt beverages (7.65): to nearest 0.1% ABV (>=0.5%); tolerance +/- 0.3 pp;
    special "low alcohol" (<2.5%), "non-alcoholic" (<0.5%, with adjacent
    statement), "alcohol free" (0.0%) rules.

  The assignment's sample field "45% Alc./Vol. (90 Proof)" is a valid distilled
  spirits statement (45% ABV + 90 proof).

  Implication: the tool must recognize multiple valid formats and normalize
  them to a canonical ABV number, then apply the tolerance. Proof should be
  cross-checked (proof = 2 x ABV).

2.4.4 NET CONTENTS
  Distilled spirits (5.70): volume as a net contents statement; "liter"/
  "litre"/"L"; "milliliters"/"ml."/"mL."/"ML."; U.S. customary and metric
  equivalents may appear in the same field of vision. Net contents may be
  blown/embossed/molded into glass.

  Wine (4.37): metric standards of fill per 4.72 (750 mL, 1.5 L, etc.);
  U.S. equivalents optional.

  Malt beverages (7.70): U.S. customary units (fl oz, pint, quart, gallon);
  metric optional and only in addition.

  Implication: normalize to a canonical volume (e.g. mL) and compare. "750 mL"
  vs "750ml" vs "750 ml" should all normalize equal.

2.4.5 NAME AND ADDRESS (producer / bottler / importer)
  - Must state the person and the city/state (address) where the operation
    occurred or principal place of business (5.66, 4.35, 7.66).
  - Preceded by a function phrase: "bottled by", "distilled by", "produced by",
    "imported by", etc., as appropriate (5.66(b), 4.35, 7.66).
  - Imported products: "imported by" + importer name/address (5.68, 4.35(b),
    7.68); different rules for imported-then-bottled vs imported-in-container.
  - Trade/operating name must match the permit/notice (5.66(d), 4.35(d),
    7.66(h)).
  - Implication: compare normalized name and city/state; the function phrase
    is significant but has acceptable synonyms.

2.4.6 COUNTRY OF ORIGIN (imports)
  - TTB defers the country-of-origin MARKING requirement to U.S. Customs and
    Border Protection: 19 CFR Parts 102 and 134 (5.69, 4.35(e), 7.69).
  - The assignment lists "Country of origin for imports" as a common element.
  - Implication: for the prototype, treat country of origin as an extractable
    field for imports and compare against the application; the precise marking
    rules live outside TTB's own CFR parts (a scope boundary worth noting).

2.5 FORMAT AND PLACEMENT RULES (APPLY TO ALL MANDATORY FIELDS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.5.1 Legibility and contrast (5.52, 4.38, 7.53)
  > Mandatory information must be "readily legible under ordinary conditions"
  > and appear on a contrasting background.
  Acceptable contrast examples (5.52(c)): black on white/cream, or white/cream
  on black. (Net contents blown into glass need not contrast.)

2.5.2 Type size
  Distilled spirits (5.53): >= 2 mm (containers > 200 mL) or >= 1 mm (<=200 mL).
  Wine (4.38): >= 2 mm (>187 mL) or >= 1 mm (<=187 mL); alcohol content between
  1 and 3 mm.
  Malt beverages (7.53): analogous 2 mm / 1 mm thresholds.
  Health warning: its own size scale (see section 3).

2.5.3 "Separate and apart"
  Mandatory information (except brand name) must be "separate and apart" from
  additional information (5.52(b)), with limited exceptions for brief optional
  phrases. Ingredient disclosures (5.63(c)) may not include additional info.

2.5.4 Language
  Mandatory information in English, except the brand name (5.55). Spanish
  permitted for Puerto Rico consumption. Foreign-language translations allowed
  if non-conflicting.

2.5.5 Placement / field of vision
  Distilled spirits: brand name, class/type, and alcohol content must share one
  field of vision (5.63(a)); name/address and net contents may be anywhere.
  Wine: brand name + class/type on the brand (front) label (4.32(a)).
  Malt beverages: all mandatory info on "a label or labels" (7.63(a)).

2.5.6 Firmly affixed
  Labels must be affixed so they cannot be removed without water/solvents
  (5.51, 4.38(e), 16.22(c)).

2.6 REGULATORY SCOPE BOUNDARIES (WHAT THE PROTOTYPE DOES NOT NEED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - COLA application submission / COLAs Online integration: explicitly out of
    scope per the assignment (standalone proof-of-concept).
  - Standards of fill (subpart K of Part 5; 4.72 for wine) govern the legal
    bottle SIZES, not the label text; only relevant to net-contents validation.
  - Advertising rules (subpart N of each part) govern ads, not labels; out of
    scope.
  - Permits, formulas, tax (Parts 1, 19, 25, 26, 27, 28) are out of scope.
  - Penalty provisions (16.33, labeling act penalty) are background, not a
    feature.

2.7 WHAT "VERIFYING A LABEL" MEANS, REDUCED TO RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The tool's core job reduces to a comparison engine with per-field semantics:

  FIELD            MATCHING SEMANTIC
  --------------   -----------------------------------------------------------
  Brand name       Normalized string compare (case/punct-tolerant); matched
                   against application Item 6.
  Class/type       Extracted from label; optional controlled-vocabulary check
                   (subpart I). Not an application field.
  Alcohol content  Parse to canonical ABV number from any legal format; check
                   legal format + tolerance; cross-check proof = 2 x ABV. Not
                   an application field.
  Net contents     Parse to canonical volume (mL); compare equal (unit-
                   tolerant); legal standard-of-fill check optional. Not an
                   application field.
  Name/address     Normalized compare vs application Item 8; function-phrase
                   synonyms allowed.
  Country of       Normalized compare (imports); CBP 19 CFR 102/134 nuance
  origin           noted. Matched against application Item 3 (domestic/imported)
                   and, if asserted, the origin string.
  Government       EXACT text + format checks (all caps bold "GOVERNMENT
  warning          WARNING:", correct wording). See section 3.
  Presence/format  Each required field must EXIST and be legible/contrasting/
                   minimum size/English/separate-and-apart. These are checks,
                   not just text comparisons.

Two matching philosophies coexist and must not be conflated:
  - FUZZY/NORMALIZED matching for values where the regulation tolerates
    formatting variation (brand name case, alcohol format, net contents units).
  - EXACT matching for the government warning, where wording is word-for-word
    mandatory and case/bold are themselves requirements.

================================================================================
3. GOVERNMENT HEALTH WARNING STATEMENT - EXACT REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================
Source: 27 CFR Part 16 (Alcoholic Beverage Health Warning Statement),
implementing the Alcoholic Beverage Labeling Act of 1988. Applies to ALL
alcohol beverages at >= 0.5% alcohol by volume. Verbatim text quoted with "> ".

This field is the hardest verification case because it is word-for-word
mandatory AND its formatting (caps, bold) is itself a requirement. The
junior-agent interview flagged exactly this: title-case "Government Warning"
instead of all caps was a rejection.

3.1 THE EXACT REQUIRED TEXT (27 CFR 16.21)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The regulation states there shall be stated, on the brand label or separate
front label, or on a back or side label, SEPARATE AND APART from all other
information, the following statement:

  > GOVERNMENT WARNING: (1) According to the Surgeon General, women should not
  > drink alcoholic beverages during pregnancy because of the risk of birth
  > defects. (2) Consumption of alcoholic beverages impairs your ability to
  > drive a car or operate machinery, and may cause health problems.

Notes for a faithful implementation:
  - The label text is the two numbered sentences joined, with "(1)" and "(2)"
    markers, a period after "birth defects." then the "(2) ..." sentence.
    On physical labels the two sentences are often on separate lines, but the
    words and punctuation are identical.
  - Exact punctuation: colon after "WARNING", periods at the end of each
    numbered sentence, comma after "drive a car or operate machinery".
  - No "and" between the two sentences; no extra words.

3.2 FORMAT REQUIREMENTS (27 CFR 16.22)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
> 16.22(a) Legibility.
>  (1) Readily legible under ordinary conditions; on a contrasting background.
>  (2) The first two words, i.e. "GOVERNMENT WARNING," shall appear in capital
>      letters and in bold type. The REMAINDER of the warning statement may not
>      appear in bold type.
>  (3) Letters/words shall not be compressed in a manner that makes the warning
>      not readily legible.
>  (4) Maximum number of characters per inch, as follows:

    Minimum required type size      Maximum characters per inch
    ----------------------------    ---------------------------
    1 millimeter                    40
    2 millimeters                   25
    3 millimeters                   12

> 16.22(b) Size of type.
>  (1) Containers <= 237 mL (8 fl. oz.): type not smaller than 1 millimeter.
>  (2) Containers > 237 mL up to 3 L (101 fl. oz.): not smaller than 2 mm.
>  (3) Containers > 3 L (101 fl. oz.): not smaller than 3 mm.

> 16.22(c) Labels firmly affixed. Labels bearing the statement that are not an
> integral part of the container must be affixed so they cannot be removed
> without thorough application of water or other solvents.

3.3 WHY THIS IS THE HARD CASE FOR THE TOOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three separable checks, of increasing difficulty:

  (1) PRESENCE: does the label carry the warning at all? (Binary; easy.)

  (2) WORDING (exact): does the extracted text match the canonical statement
      word-for-word? Must tolerate OCR character errors but must NOT tolerate
      real deviations. Correct approach: normalized edit-distance / fuzzy-match
      against the single canonical string with a tight threshold, then surface
      near-misses for human review rather than silently passing or failing.
      Any wording change (e.g. dropped "(2)", "Government Warning" case change)
      is a rejection in the real workflow.

  (3) FORMAT: is "GOVERNMENT WARNING:" (with colon) in all caps and bold, and
      the remainder NOT bold? On a contrasting background, sufficient size?
      Detecting BOLD from an image is feasible but not perfectly reliable
      (font-weight estimation). Caps are detectable via OCR case. Font size/
      contrast are estimable but approximate.

  Recommended stance for the prototype:
      - Implement (1) and (2) as deterministic checks.
      - For (3), detect the all-caps property reliably (string-level), and
        report bold/size/contrast as BEST-EFFORT heuristic flags with an
        explicit "human confirm" indicator, since pixel-based bold and font-
        size detection is unreliable and the cost of a false negative (missing
        a real violation) is high.

3.4 VERIFICATION SEMANTIC SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Canonical string (single source of truth), stored verbatim.
  - Comparison: normalize whitespace/newlines; allow OCR-confusable character
    substitutions only via fuzzy matching; report a confidence + diff.
  - The "GOVERNMENT WARNING:" prefix must be all-caps in the extracted text
    (title-case or mixed-case = flag).
  - Do not rely on a generative model to "remember" the text: always compare
    against the canonical string deterministically (see section 5).

3.5 IMPLEMENTATION STATUS NOTE 
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KNOWN GAP - the current implementation is NOT up to spec here. The labeld
verification engine checks only warning PRESENCE, WORDING, and the all-caps
"GOVERNMENT WARNING:" prefix. The 27 CFR 16.22 FORMAT requirements are not
implemented at all:

  - bold on the first two words / non-bold remainder (16.22(a)(2));
  - minimum type size by container volume (16.22(b));
  - maximum characters per inch (16.22(a)(4));
  - legibility and contrasting background (16.22(a)(1));
  - "separate and apart" from other information (16.21), plus the analogous
    legibility/contrast/type-size rules in 5.52-5.53, 4.38, and 7.53.

This falls short of sections 3.3, 7.6.7(c), and 7.6.8, which call for these to
be emitted as best-effort heuristic flags with a "human confirm" indicator. A
label whose warning string is correct but printed in the wrong size, non-bold,
or low contrast is currently reported Approved with no signal that those checks
were never run. Accepted as a prototype gap (pixel-based bold/size detection is
unreliable without a scale reference, per 8.4), but recorded for follow-up.

4. THE EXISTING COLA SYSTEM AND DATA MODEL
================================================================================

4.1 HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The "form" is TTB F 5100.31 - Application for and Certification/Exemption of
Label/Bottle Approval (COLA). Two submission channels:

  - COLAs Online (since 2003) - the dominant path. Fields are typed into a web
    form (checkboxes + text boxes); label artwork is uploaded as images/PDF.
    Structured input, not an image.
  - Paper filing - legacy path. Applicant prints/types the form, signs in ink,
    and physically affixes the labels (glue/tape, "DO NOT STAPLE").

COLAs Online is a digital mirror of Form 5100.31 (help docs: "Create an
Application", "View Application Detail", "Printable e-filed COLA", "Printable
paper-filed COLA"). The Public COLA Registry exposes approved COLA records for
read-only search.

4.2 THE FORM'S FIELD LIST (TTB F 5100.31, items 1-20)

  3      Source of Product: Domestic/Imported    yes
  4      Serial Number (e.g. "24-123")           yes
  5      Type of Product: Wine / Distilled
         Spirits / Malt Beverages                yes

CRITICAL FINDING - what is NOT on the form:
  The 5100.31 has no field for alcohol content, net contents, class/type
  designation, or the government warning. Item 5 is only the commodity category
  (wine/spirits/malt), NOT the class/type like "Kentucky Straight Bourbon
  Whiskey". ABV, net contents, class/type, and the warning are verified from
  the label artwork against the regulations, not matched to a form value. The
  form's only label-matchable free-text fields are brand name (Item 6) and
  applicant name/address (Item 8).

  This contradicts a simplification in the take-home: its "Sample Label" lists
  Brand Name, Class/Type, Alcohol Content, Net Contents, Government Warning as
  if they were application fields. In the real system, four of those five are
  label-only. See section 4.7.

4.3 THE E-APPLICATION STATUS VOCABULARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
From COLAs Online FAQ C22/C23, an e-application's status is one of:

  Received, Approved, Conditionally Approved, Needs Correction, Rejected

  - "Needs Correction" = TTB cannot approve as submitted; intermediate step
    giving the submitter a chance to bring the application into compliance.
  - "Rejected" = final adverse action.
  - "Conditionally Approved" = a newer intermediate state.

This vocabulary is directly reusable for the prototype's output verdicts
(section 4.8).

4.4 THE LABEL-IMAGE FILE CONTRACT (FAQ C28-C32)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the interoperability gold - the exact input contract COLAs Online
enforces for label images:

  - Format: JPEG (.jpg, .jpeg) or PNG (.png) ONLY. TIFF is rejected.
  - Size: no single label image may exceed 1.5 MB.
  - Resolution: at least 120-170 dpi recommended; JPEG quality "Medium"
    (7/10 or 70/100).
  - One file per label surface: brand, back, neck, etc., each uploaded as a
    separate file.
  - Cropped to the label itself: "the edges of the image just touch the label"
    (crop out all background/white space).
  - Attachments (letterhead, organic cert): DOC (.doc/.docx), TEXT (.txt),
    PDF (.pdf), or JPEG (.jpg/.jpeg/.jpe).

4.5 THE INTEROPERABILITY CEILING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - No public API. COLAs Online lives at ttbonline.gov behind registration,
    MFA, and third-party-filer roles. The Public COLA Registry is read-only
    search and is bot-protected (Akamai CAPTCHA; not programmatically
    accessible without a human).
  - Therefore "interoperate" is achievable as SCHEMA/CONTRACT PARITY, not as
    "call a COLA API": a real COLAs Online e-application could be mapped into
    the JSON below with a thin converter, and back out, with no semantic drift.
  - The assignment itself says COLA integration is out of scope; this schema
    merely keeps the door open for a future procurement/workflow integration.

4.7 THE SEMANTIC GAP 
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The 5100.31 mirror has no field for alcohol content, net contents, class/type,
or the warning - because the real form does not either. The only things the
application side actually asserts that you can match against the label are:

  - Brand name (Item 6)  -> label brand name
  - Name & address (Item 8) -> label "bottled by / imported by" statement
  - Product type (Item 5) and Source (Item 3) -> which regulatory part applies

So the faithful contract is: ABV, net contents, class/type, and the warning
are DERIVED from `labels[]` and validated against 27 CFR 4/5/7/16 - not matched
to a form field. That is how the real review works.

If the take-home's richer "expected values" are wanted (a place to paste
expected ABV/net-contents/class-type), keep them in a SEPARATE, clearly-marked
extension block (`expected` above), so the `application` object stays a pure
5100.31 mirror and the extension does not pollute interoperability.

  PASS  -> "Approved"

("Conditionally Approved" exists too but is an e-application lifecycle state,

================================================================================
5. IMAGE / TEXT EXTRACTION - PROCESSING MODEL OPTIONS
================================================================================
Scope: how to turn a photographed label into (a) structured field values and
(b) the exact government-warning text, within the ~5 second budget, tolerating
imperfect photos (angles, glare, bad lighting). Secondary to the regulations
but central to feasibility.

The task splits into two different cognitive jobs, and no single model is best
at both:

  JOB A - DETERMINISTIC TEXT EXTRACTION (OCR). Read the characters/words and
  where they are. Needs to be FAST and CHARACTER-ACCURATE. This is what must
  feed the exact government-warning comparison.

  JOB B - STRUCTURAL UNDERSTANDING (which text is which field). On a decorated
  label, "45% Alc./Vol." is alcohol content, "750 mL" is net contents, "OLD TOM
  DISTILLERY" is the brand. Needs LAYOUT + SEMANTIC reasoning, which is where
  multimodal LLMs shine (and plain OCR is weak).

5.1 OPTION CLASSES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1.1 MANAGED CLOUD OCR (deterministic)  [DECISION: Textract raw OCR]
  - AWS Textract DetectDocumentText (raw/pure text OCR) - SELECTED. Returns
    lines/words with bounding boxes and per-word confidence; no form/table
    structure. Synchronous, sub-5s for a single page, ~$1.50/1,000 pages.
    Deterministic, cheap, no hallucination.
  - AWS Textract AnalyzeDocument (forms/tables key-value) - CLOSED. Costs
    ~$50-65/1,000 pages and needs predictable layouts; weak on decorative,
    free-form bottle labels. The pure text path is sufficient.
  - Google Cloud Document AI / Vision OCR - CLOSED (second cloud + cross-cloud
    dependency; no advantage for this scope).
  - Azure AI Document Intelligence (formerly Form Recognizer) - CLOSED (TTB is
    on Azure today, but this prototype is scoped single-cloud AWS).
  Strengths (of the selected option): fast, deterministic, cheap, no
  hallucination. Weaknesses: no semantic sense of "which text is the brand
  name" on a non-form layout; degraded on rotated/glared images without
  pre-processing (mitigated by the Bedrock criteria + pre-processing).

5.1.2 MANAGED MULTIMODAL LLM (vision)  [DECISION: AWS Bedrock - REJECTED for the prototype; see 5.5]
  - Amazon Bedrock: hosted Claude (Anthropic) and Amazon Nova Pro/Lite vision
    models - originally SELECTED, now REJECTED for the prototype (see 5.5):
    the shipped engine is 100% deterministic and no Bedrock call is
    implemented. Used NOT for extraction but for the LLM Boolean criteria
    (7.6.9): field disambiguation, class/type conformance, function-phrase
    equivalence. Receives OCR text, returns strict-JSON verdicts; never
    transcribes.
  - OpenAI GPT-4o / GPT-4.1 (vision), Google Gemini (vision) - CLOSED (external
    vendors; the single-cloud AWS posture rules them out).
  Strengths: layout robustness, natural field identification, glare/angle
  tolerance, structured JSON output. Weaknesses: (a) LATENCY often 3-10s+,
  which can violate the 5-second budget - mitigated by running IN PARALLEL with
  the deterministic criteria and bounding with a timeout; (b) COST higher
  per-call; (c) HALLUCINATION may "correct" or invent characters - fatal for
  word-for-word warning verification, so the LLM never transcribes;
  (d) non-determinism.

5.1.3 SELF-HOSTED OPEN SOURCE  [CLOSED for the prototype]
  - PaddleOCR, Tesseract 5, EasyOCR, DeepSeek-OCR, and self-hosted vision LLMs
    (Qwen2.5-VL, Llama-Vision) are all viable for a future production
    deployment needing data sovereignty, but are CLOSED for this prototype:
    they require operating GPU infrastructure and model serving, out of scope.
    Retained only as the documented fallback (see 5.4).
  Strengths: no per-call cost, full data control (federal-friendly), no outbound
  dependency on third-party endpoints. Weaknesses: you own the infrastructure
  (GPU), model serving, and updates.

5.2 THE ARCHITECTURAL INSIGHT (IMPORTANT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Do not let a generative model near exact-string verification. Division of labor:

  (1) Deterministic OCR extracts ALL text with positions and per-character
      confidence (Job A). Fast and reproducible.
  (2) A field-mapping step assigns text to fields (brand, class/type, ABV, net
      contents, name/address, origin, warning). This can be:
        - rule/heuristic (regex on ABV/volume patterns, position-based), or
        - a multimodal LLM that receives the OCR text (NOT trusted to
          transcribe) and outputs field assignments.
  (3) Verification is a CONJUNCTION of Boolean criteria (see 7.6.9):
        - deterministic fuzzy-string criteria (normalize + compare; edit-
          distance against the canonical warning string), AND
        - AWS Bedrock LLM criteria for checks that need semantic judgment
          (class/type conformance, brand-vs-marketing-copy disambiguation).
      The LLM never transcribes and never decides "match vs mismatch" for the
      exact warning wording or for ABV numbers.

Why: an LLM transcribing "GOVERNMENT WARNING: (1) According to the Surgeon
General..." will occasionally drop or reword a token. A deterministic OCR
string compared against the canonical text cannot "rewrite" the label.

  ---------------------------   -------   --------  ---------   -------  -----------
  AWS Textract (raw OCR)        ~1-3s     High      $/page      None     AWS only

  Google Document AI            ~1-3s     Very high $/page      None     Google

5.4 DECISION 
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXTRACTION: AWS Textract DetectDocumentText (raw/pure text OCR) is the single
extractor for the initial text-extraction step. CLOSED: Textract AnalyzeDocument
(forms/tables), Google Document AI, Azure Document Intelligence, and all
self-hosted OCR (PaddleOCR/Tesseract/DeepSeek-OCR). Pure text extraction is
chosen over document/forms analysis because the label is a decorative free-form
surface, not a form; raw OCR is faster, cheaper, and sufficient - structure is
recovered by the field-mapping and verification stages, not by the OCR vendor.

Field mapping (which text is which field): regex/heuristics first, resolved
deterministically. A Bedrock LLM criterion was ORIGINALLY proposed for
uncertain cases (7.6.9) but is REJECTED for the prototype (see 5.5): residual
uncertainty is handled by position/qualifier/layout heuristics and, where
unresolved, surfaced as Needs Correction for human review rather than answered
by a generative model.

Firewall: because all processing runs server-side inside AWS, the TTB client's
outbound firewall only needs to reach the app's own HTTPS endpoint - it never
needs to reach Textract or Bedrock directly. This neutralizes the exact failure
that killed the prior vendor pilot. (See section 6.)

Data-sovereignty fallback (documented, NOT prototype): PaddleOCR on a GPU EC2
instance for a future production review if managed-service image transfer is
ever disallowed.

5.5 BEDROCK LLM CRITERIA - REJECTED FOR THE PROTOTYPE (RESOLVED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Finding (post-implementation): the prototype does not need the Bedrock LLM.
The shipped verification engine is 100% deterministic - every criterion in
verify.go is normalization, regex, or edit-distance; there is no
bedrock-runtime dependency, no bedrock:InvokeModel IAM grant, and no LLM call
anywhere in the code path. The Bedrock references that remain in this document
(5.1.2, 5.4, 6.x, 7.6.9) are design notes, not behavior. OCR is Textract
DetectDocumentText (deterministic), not an LLM.

Rationale - why pure determinism + Textract suffices:

  (1) The problem itself is matching, not reasoning. Per the deputy-director
      interview ("just... matching... the number on the form is the same as
      the number on the label"), every acceptance criterion (7.9) is a
      string/number comparison already realized by a deterministic check:
      case/punct-tolerant brand match, canonical ABV + tolerance, canonical
      mL net contents, function-phrase synonyms, and exact/tight-edit warning
      wording. None of these is LLM-shaped.

  (2) The criteria originally reserved for Bedrock (7.6.9 class B) each have
      a deterministic resolution:
        - class/type conformance: subpart I is a FINITE controlled vocabulary,
          so it is a lookup, not an open-ended judgment call;
        - brand-vs-marketing-copy disambiguation: position/qualifier/layout
          heuristics (e.g. the ABV qualifier window) handle this; residual
          ambiguity should degrade to Needs Correction, not to a model;
        - function-phrase equivalence: already regex synonyms.

  (3) Latency: the hard 5s budget (1.4) and this doc's own comparison table
      (5.3) place Bedrock vision at 3-10s+. "Run in parallel + timeout" means
      a slow call discards its own signal by degrading to Needs Correction -
      paying per-call for a verdict you throw away. Deterministic checks are
      ~0ms.

  (4) Determinism and auditability: a compliance verdict must be reproducible
      and regression-testable. A generative criterion is non-deterministic and
      hard to test; the evaluation rubric rewards scope-appropriate choices,
      and for this scope the appropriate choice is deterministic.

  (5) Hallucination boundary: this doc already confines the LLM away from the
      exact-string fields (warning wording, ABV) because it can "correct" or
      invent characters (5.1.2(c), 5.2). Those are the fields that matter most
      and are already exact/deterministic; the residual "judgment" fields are
      the least load-bearing.

  (6) Surface and cost: Bedrock adds a second AWS service (credential, IAM
      grant, per-call cost, timeout machinery) for criteria no acceptance test
      requires. Textract is already in place for extraction.

Decision: DROP Bedrock for the prototype. Verification is deterministic
fuzzy-string + Textract only. Where an LLM could genuinely earn its keep
(semantic field disambiguation at scale on decorative/glared labels), defer it
behind the deterministic field-mapping heuristics; revisit only if a future
requirement demands judgment beyond a controlled vocabulary. Jenny's
imperfect-image ask (1.4) is an OCR/pre-processing robustness problem,
not an LLM problem.

6. DEPLOYMENT AND ARCHITECTURE (SINGLE-CLOUD AWS)
================================================================================
Scope: how to host a standalone prototype backend (likely Go) and the image-
processing path on AWS, meeting the hard constraints: ~5s per-label synchronous
results, 200-300 label batch uploads, and the TTB firewall situation.

6.1 WHY SINGLE-CLOUD AWS ADDRESSES THE FIREWALL PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The IT-admin interview is decisive:

  > "Our network blocks outbound traffic to a lot of domains... During the
  > scanning vendor pilot, half their features didn't work because our firewall
  > blocked connections to their ML endpoints."

Two ways this bites:

  (a) CLIENT-SIDE processing (OCR in the browser / on TTB desktops calling
      external ML endpoints) is the wrong shape - the TTB network will block
      the endpoints, exactly as it did before.

  (b) SERVER-SIDE processing inside one cloud is the right shape: the browser
      talks HTTPS only to the app's own AWS endpoint; the app then calls AWS
      services (Textract/Bedrock/S3) over AWS's own network. The client
      firewall sees only one whitelistable domain. No third-party ML endpoints
      in the client path.

So a single cloud (AWS) is not merely a preference; it is the design that
survives the stated network constraint. Any external model vendor (OpenAI,
Google, Azure) used from the backend also stays off the client network, but
concentrating on AWS avoids cross-cloud egress, credential sprawl, and
vendor-to-vendor latency.

6.2 COMPUTE OPTIONS  [DECISION: single Go monolith]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The backend is ONE Go process (monolith) with a goroutine per in-flight label,
deployed on one instance - a single ECS Fargate task or a small EC2 instance.
Rationale: the process is I/O-bound waiting on Textract/Bedrock, so its own
capacity is irrelevant; one process with goroutines is simpler and cheaper than
a serverless fan-out (see 6.7 for the worst-case numbers).

  Option              Fit
  -----------------   ---------------------------------------------------------
  ECS on Fargate      Containerized Go monolith, no servers to
                      manage. One task is plenty; it only orchestrates
                      Textract/Bedrock calls.
  EC2 [CHOSEN]        Equivalent alternative if a persistent box is preferred.
                      GPU only if self-hosting OCR (out of scope - 5.4).
  AWS Lambda          REJECTED for this workload: bills wall-clock duration, so
                      it pays for the seconds spent blocked waiting on
                      Textract/Bedrock (see 6.7.3).
  EKS (Kubernetes)    Overkill for a prototype; justified only at scale.
  Managed ML          AWS Textract (OCR) and Bedrock (LLM) carry the real work
                      and need no compute you manage.

  Baseline: single Go monolith on one ECS Fargate task (or small EC2 instance);
  image storage in S3; OCR via Textract (DetectDocumentText); LLM Boolean
  criteria via Bedrock in parallel goroutines; batch via SQS + goroutine pool
  (see 6.3.2).
  Fallback: Go backend + PaddleOCR on a GPU EC2 if a future requirement forbids
  sending label images to a managed service.

6.3 THE TWO PROCESSING SHAPES: SYNC (5s) AND ASYNC (BATCH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  6.3.1 SYNC PATH (single label, the ~5s guarantee)
      Browser -> HTTPS -> API -> Textract DetectDocumentText (1-3s) ->
      fan out: ALL verification criteria run IN PARALLEL (deterministic fuzzy
      criteria + Bedrock LLM criteria) -> AND the results -> JSON -> browser.
      The OCR step is the only serial gate; every criterion downstream is
      independent and runs concurrently, so verification latency is
      max(criterion latency), not the sum. Bedrock criteria are bounded (strict
      JSON schema, timeout) so a slow generative call degrades that one
      criterion to Needs Correction rather than blowing the 5s budget.

  6.3.2 ASYNC PATH (200-300 labels, peak-season importers)
      Browser -> multipart upload -> S3 -> SQS message per image (durability/
      retry) -> the SAME monolith drains the queue with a bounded goroutine
      pool (same OCR + parallel verify per image) -> results persisted ->
      browser polls status / receives results.
      Requirements: job id, per-label status, partial-progress reporting,
      retry on transient OCR failure, and a results summary (pass/fail/flag
      per label + count). Batch is a first-class feature, not an afterthought.

6.4 DATA FLOW (SINGLE CLOUD)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Browser (TTB agent)
      |  HTTPS (single AWS domain)
      v
  API (Go monolith, single instance)
      |  -> S3 (store uploaded label images; prototype: transient storage)
      |  -> Textract DetectDocumentText (OCR) [managed, in-AWS, serial gate]
      |  -> Bedrock (LLM Boolean criteria)    [managed, in-AWS, parallel]
      v
  Verification engine (Go): N independent Boolean criteria (deterministic
  fuzzy-match + Bedrock LLM) evaluated in PARALLEL, then AND-ed -> per-field
  verdicts + confidence -> JSON
      v
  Browser renders verdicts (clean, obvious - see section 7)

6.5 SECURITY / FEDERAL NOTES (PROTOTYPE SCOPE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - The assignment says: for a prototype, "don't do anything crazy" and "we're
    not storing anything sensitive for this exercise."
  - Therefore: no PII retention, transient S3 objects with lifecycle deletion,
    TLS everywhere, minimal IAM (least privilege), and NO FedRAMP posture work
    for the prototype (FedRAMP is a production-phase concern - the 2019 Azure
    migration took 18 months of certification paperwork).
  - Document the gap: a production deployment would need a formal security
    review (PII, document retention, FedRAMP) that the prototype defers.
  - The evaluation criteria reward "appropriate technical choices for the
    scope" - a clean, small, single-cloud deployment is more credible than an
    over-built one.

6.6 SCALING NOTE 
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ~150k applications/year and heavy 200-300-label seasonal bursts are well
  within a SINGLE Go monolith: the burst translates to at most a few thousand
  concurrent in-flight images, which goroutines absorb (see 6.7). The "5
  seconds" requirement bounds the SYNC path; the BATCH path can be nearline
  (seconds to a minute for a full batch) without violating the UX constraint.
  Horizontal scale (a second instance behind a load balancer + a managed queue)
  is a production-only option, not needed at prototype traffic.

6.7 WORST-CASE LOAD ANALYSIS AND THE MONOLITH DECISION (RESOLVED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Decision: the backend is a SINGLE Go monolith - one process, many goroutines -
on one instance (a modest EC2 instance or a single ECS Fargate task). NOT
Lambda, NOT a worker fleet. The worst-case load model below is why.

6.7.1 WORST-CASE LOAD MODEL
  Throughput: ~150,000 applications/year (stakeholder). Assume 4 label images
  per application (brand/back/neck + one spare) as an upper bound:
      ~600,000 label images/year
      ~2,400 images/workday (250 days)
      ~300 images/hour (8h day)  =  ~5 images/minute average.

  Peak burst: an importer dumps 200-300 applications at once; worst case 300
  apps x 4 images = 1,200 images in one dump. Even several such dumps in the
  same peak hour is only a few thousand images/hour - single-digit images per
  second, low-double-digit under extreme assumptions.

  Concurrency: each image spends ~1-3s in Textract (sync OCR), then ~0-4s in
  parallel criteria (Bedrock bounded by timeout); worst-case dwell ~5s/image.
  To turn the largest dump (1,200 images) around in ~1 minute the monolith
  needs ~20 images/s through Textract, i.e. ~20-60 in-flight images. Even an
  extreme self-imposed cap of 1,000 concurrent in-flight images is ~1,000
  goroutines (a few MB of stacks) plus ~1.5 GB of transient image buffers. Go
  runs hundreds of thousands of goroutines in one process without strain.

6.7.2 WHY THE MONOLITH IS NOT THE BOTTLENECK
  CPU: per label the Go work is string normalization, a handful of compares,
  one edit-distance over ~500 chars, a few regexes (ABV/volume), and JSON
  marshal - microseconds to a few ms. At 100 labels/s this is <1% of one core.
  The monolith is effectively idle; ALL the real work (OCR, LLM reasoning)
  runs in Textract and Bedrock.

  Memory: per in-flight label the monolith holds only the image bytes (<=1.5 MB
  each, streamed to Textract then released) plus a few KB of OCR text and JSON.
  A few hundred concurrent labels is tens-to-hundreds of MB; even an extreme
  1,000 in-flight is ~1.5 GB of transient buffers. Realistic peaks (tens of
  in-flight images) are trivial for one instance.

  Network: outbound HTTPS to Textract and Bedrock over a pooled HTTP/2
  connection. One process reuses one connection pool; there is no per-request
  overhead a second process would relieve.

  The ONLY binding constraint is managed-service quota (Textract sync TPS,
  Bedrock RPM/TPS), which is service-side, region-dependent, and raisable. The
  monolith saturates those quotas long before its own CPU/memory/network.

6.7.3 WHY NOT LAMBDA (THE WASTE ARGUMENT)
  A synchronous label review is I/O-bound: ~3-5s of its wall clock is spent
  blocked waiting on Textract, then on Bedrock. Lambda bills wall-clock
  duration x memory, so every request pays for seconds of idle wait - roughly
  GB-seconds/year of pure waiting at prototype traffic, plus per-request cold
  starts and re-established connections. A monolith pays for instance uptime
  instead, reuses one connection pool and one process, and gives deterministic,
  debuggable behavior with zero orchestration. For this load one instance is
  both cheaper and simpler.

6.7.4 SCOPE AND LIMITS OF THIS DECISION
  - Single point of failure and instance upkeep (patching/availability) are
    accepted for a prototype. Production would add a second instance behind a
    load balancer and a managed queue - trivial horizontal scale if ever
    needed, but NOT needed at prototype traffic.
  - This is a prototype decision, not a production-capacity claim. The honest
    ceiling is Textract/Bedrock quota, which must be MEASURED, not assumed
    (see 8.6).

7. SYSTEM SPECIFICATION (ENGLISH)
================================================================================
This is the destination: a plain-English specification of what the system OUGHT
to do, derived from the assignment and the regulations (sections 2-4). It is
deliberately free of UI/UX detail (the assignment ranks UI/UX last) and free of
implementation detail beyond what is needed to define behavior.

7.1 PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A standalone tool for TTB Compliance Division label-review agents. Given (a)
one or more photographs of an alcohol beverage label and (b) the label's
application data (the values asserted on Form 5100.31), the system extracts the
mandatory label information from the image and reports, per field, whether the
label matches the application and whether the label satisfies mandatory format
requirements. Output is a clear per-label verdict an agent can act on in a few
seconds.

7.2 SCOPE AND NON-GOALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IN SCOPE (prototype):
  - Accept label images and application data.
  - Extract and verify the mandatory label fields below.
  - Single-label interactive mode (target: ~5 seconds).
  - Batch mode (200-300 labels at once).
  - Clear, obvious presentation of pass/fail/flag per field.
NON-GOALS (explicitly deferred):
  - COLA submission / COLAs Online integration.
  - Permit, formula, or tax functions.
  - Advertising review.
  - Persistent storage of sensitive data / PII (prototype uses transient data).
  - FedRAMP certification.

7.3 USERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Label-compliance agents (primary users): vary from "prints email" to
    "could build this herself"; half over 50. Must be usable by a 73-year-old
    benchmark. Clean, obvious, no hunting for buttons.
  - (Future) reviewers/supervisors who inspect batch results.
No login complexity is assumed for the prototype; identify reviewers only as
needed.

7.4 INPUTS (CORRECTED TO MIRROR THE COLA SYSTEM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  7.4.1 Label images: one or more photographs per label, one file per surface
  (brand, back, neck). Accepted formats: JPEG/PNG ONLY (reject TIFF), <= 1.5 MB
  each, 120-170 dpi recommended, cropped to the label. This mirrors the COLAs
  Online file contract (section 4.4). May be imperfect (angle, lighting, glare)
  - the system should attempt correction and degrade gracefully.

  7.4.2 Application data: a JSON object mirroring TTB Form 5100.31 (section
  4.6). The fields that participate in matching are:
        - product_type (Item 5): wine / distilled_spirits / malt_beverages
        - source (Item 3): domestic / imported
        - brand_name (Item 6)
        - applicant name & address (Item 8)
        - (optional) fanciful name (Item 7), formula (Item 9), varietals (10),
          appellation (11), application_type (Item 14)
  The government warning is NOT an application field - it is a fixed legal
  constant the system checks against the canonical text (section 3).

  7.4.3 IMPORTANT: alcohol content, net contents, class/type, and the warning
  are NOT supplied by the caller. They are DERIVED from the label images and
  validated against 27 CFR 4/5/7/16 (section 4.7). If the take-home's richer
  "expected values" are wanted, they go in a separate `expected` extension
  block, never in the `application` object.

7.5 PROCESSING PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. INGEST: accept image(s) + application data; create a review record.
  2. PRE-PROCESS (best effort): deskew, crop to label, reduce glare, normalize
     contrast, de-rotate.
  3. EXTRACT: read all text on the label with positions and per-character
     confidence (deterministic OCR).
  4. FIELD-MAP: assign extracted text to the mandatory fields (brand name,
     class/type, alcohol content, net contents, name/address, country of
     origin, government warning) using layout and pattern rules; a Bedrock
     vision model MAY assist (see the LLM criteria in 7.6.9) but never
     transcribes trusted values.
  5. VERIFY (per field, see 7.6): evaluate all Boolean criteria IN PARALLEL
     (deterministic fuzzy-string + Bedrock LLM), then AND them into the overall
     verdict.
  6. REPORT: produce per-field verdicts and an overall label verdict:
     Approved / Needs Correction / Rejected, each with the extracted text and
     the compared value so the agent can see WHY.

7.6 VERIFICATION RULES (THE CORE CONTRACT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two matching philosophies coexist and MUST NOT be conflated:
  (A) NORMALIZED matching - for fields where the regulation permits formatting
      variation. Compare canonicalized values.
  (B) EXACT matching - for the government warning, where wording is word-for-
      word mandatory and case/bold are themselves requirements.

Per-field rules:

  7.6.1 BRAND NAME - normalized compare vs application Item 6. Canonicalize
  case, whitespace, punctuation (smart quotes -> straight, apostrophes/
  possessives). A case-only difference ("STONE'S THROW" vs "Stone's Throw") is
  a MATCH. Report a mismatch only on substantive difference. Missing -> fail.

  7.6.2 CLASS/TYPE DESIGNATION - extracted from the label; normalize and,
  optionally, validate against a controlled vocabulary of legal standards of
  identity (subpart I of 27 CFR 4/5/7). Missing -> fail. (Not an application
  field; if `expected.class_type` is provided, compare against it too.)

  7.6.3 ALCOHOL CONTENT - parse to a canonical ABV number from any legal format
  and check it is a legal statement; apply tolerance where a value is asserted:
        distilled spirits: +/- 0.3 percentage points (5.65(c))
        malt beverages:   +/- 0.3 percentage points (7.65(c))
        wine:             +/- 1.0 (>14%) or 1.5 (<=14%) (4.36(b))
  If proof is stated, cross-check proof = 2 x ABV and flag if inconsistent.
  Wine <=14% ABV: content NOT required if "table"/"light" present. Malt: content
  only mandatory in the added-flavor-alcohol case (7.63(a)(3)).

  7.6.4 NET CONTENTS - parse to a canonical volume (mL) tolerating unit
  variants ("750 mL", "750ml", "750 ml", "25.4 fl. oz."). Check presence and
  legal standard of fill (optional). Missing -> fail. (Not an application
  field; if `expected.net_contents` is provided, compare equal.)

  7.6.5 NAME AND ADDRESS - normalized compare vs application Item 8 (name +
  city/state). Function-phrase synonyms ("bottled by", "distilled by",
  "produced by", "imported by") are equivalent in spirit; correct function
  phrase + matching name+city/state is the pass condition. Missing -> fail.

  7.6.6 COUNTRY OF ORIGIN (imports only) - if application Item 3 is "imported",
  extract and normalize the origin; compare if asserted. Note the precise
  marking rules live in 19 CFR 102/134 (CBP), not TTB's own parts. Missing on
  an import -> fail.

  7.6.7 GOVERNMENT WARNING - EXACT + format (section 3):
      (a) PRESENCE required (all beverages >= 0.5% ABV) -> fail if absent.
      (b) WORDING: fuzzy edit-distance against the canonical string with a
          tight threshold; tolerate ONLY OCR noise, never real deviation.
          Any wording change (dropped/reordered token, case change of
          "GOVERNMENT WARNING", missing "(2)") -> flag/fail.
      (c) FORMAT: "GOVERNMENT WARNING:" must be all-caps (string check - do
          reliably); bold, font size, and contrast reported as BEST-EFFORT
          heuristic flags with a "human confirm" indicator. Title-case
          "Government Warning" must be flagged (real rejection in workflow).

  7.6.8 PRESENCE/FORMAT (all mandatory fields) - each required field must EXIST
  and be legible/contrasting/English/minimum size/separate-and-apart per
  section 2.5. Presence is a hard check; legibility, contrast, and type size
  are best-effort heuristic flags.

  7.6.9 VERIFICATION AS A CONJUNCTION OF PARALLEL BOOLEAN CRITERIA
  Every per-field rule (7.6.1-7.6.8) is realized as one or more BOOLEAN
  criteria. Each criterion is a pure function: given the OCR output and the
  application data, it returns PASS (true) or FAIL (false), plus evidence
  (extracted value, compared value, confidence, reason). No criterion holds
  mutable state, so they are trivially parallelizable.

  OVERALL VERDICT IS A PURE CONJUNCTION (AND):
      label = Approved  <=>  EVERY criterion returns PASS
      any single FAIL  =>  label is NOT Approved (Rejected for a hard/missing
                           failure; Needs Correction for a near-threshold or
                           low-confidence result - see 7.7).

  All criteria are DETERMINISTIC FUZZY-STRING CHECKS (no model call):
      Normalize both sides, then compare. "Fuzzy" here means mechanical
      normalization, NOT model-based similarity: lower-case, collapse
      whitespace, strip/normalize punctuation, map synonyms (function phrases),
      parse to a canonical number with a tolerance. Used for:
        - brand name (case/punct-tolerant),
        - net contents (canonical volume),
        - alcohol content (canonical ABV + tolerance + proof cross-check),
        - name & address (normalized + function-phrase synonyms),
        - country of origin (normalized),
        - warning PRESENCE and WORDING (tight edit-distance vs the canonical
          string; all-caps prefix check).
      These are the cheap, fast, reproducible checks. There is no Bedrock LLM
      criterion: class/type conformance, field disambiguation, and function-
      phrase equivalence resolve deterministically (see 5.5 and 8.17).

  PARALLELISM (the latency argument):
      All criteria are INDEPENDENT given the OCR output, so they are evaluated
      CONCURRENTLY after the single serial OCR step. Verification latency is
      max(criterion latency), not the sum, which keeps the ~5s budget intact.

7.7 OUTPUT / VERDICT SEMANTICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Per field, report at minimum:
      field name | extracted value | application/expected value | verdict
      (Approved / Needs Correction / Rejected) | confidence | brief reason
  Overall label verdict:
      Approved         - ALL Boolean criteria pass (conjunction, 7.6.9): every
                         required field present and matching; warning exact and
                         correctly formatted (within limits).
      Needs Correction - normalized compare is within tolerance of a boundary,
                         OCR confidence is low, or a best-effort format
                         heuristic (bold/size/contrast) is inconclusive -
                         needs a human to confirm.
      Rejected         - a required field is missing, or a deterministic exact
                         check (warning wording/case) failed.
  The agent must always see the extracted text side by side with the expected
  value; never present a bare verdict without evidence.

7.8 NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  7.8.1 LATENCY: single-label interactive results in about 5 seconds (hard;
  the prior 30-40s pilot was abandoned). Any slow optional step (vision LLM)
  must not sit on the critical path.
  7.8.2 BATCH: accept 200-300 labels at once; process asynchronously with job
  status, per-label progress, and a summary (counts per verdict). Batch may be
  nearline (seconds-to-a-minute) without violating UX.
  7.8.3 USABILITY: clean and obvious; no hunting for buttons; large, plain
  verdicts; assume the 73-year-old benchmark. Errors and near-misses explained
  in plain language.
  7.8.4 CORRECTNESS: deterministic verification for exact fields; no generative
  model decides match/mismatch on the warning or on ABV numbers.
  7.8.5 SECURITY (prototype): no sensitive-data retention; transient image
  storage with deletion; TLS in transit. Formal PII/retention/FedRAMP review is
  a documented production-phase gap, not a prototype feature.
  7.8.6 ROBUSTNESS: handle imperfect images (angle/glare/lighting) by pre-
  processing and, where extraction confidence is low, return "Needs Correction"
  with the low-confidence region highlighted rather than silently failing.

7.9 ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA (HOW TO KNOW IT WORKS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Given the sample distilled-spirits label (brand "OLD TOM DISTILLERY",
    class/type "Kentucky Straight Bourbon Whiskey", "45% Alc./Vol. (90 Proof)",
    "750 mL", standard warning), the system returns Approved when the
    application matches and correctly flags each field when it does not.
  - A case-only brand-name difference (STONE'S THROW vs Stone's Throw) is
    reported as a MATCH (not a false failure).
  - A title-case "Government Warning" (or any wording deviation) is flagged.
  - "45% Alc./Vol." and "Alc 45% by vol" normalize to the same ABV and pass.
  - "750 mL", "750ml", and "25.4 fl oz" normalize to the same net contents.
  - Single-label result returns within ~5 seconds.
  - A 250-label batch completes with per-label status and a summary, without
    blocking the interactive path.
  - A label missing any mandatory field returns Rejected naming the field.

8. UNCERTAINTIES, OPEN QUESTIONS, AND RISKS
================================================================================
Consolidated list of everything that could not be resolved from the assignment
or the regulations, plus explicit risks. Each item states the question, why it
matters, and a recommended default so work can proceed without re-litigating.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 BEVERAGE-TYPE SCOPE
Q: Should the prototype cover all three beverage types or start with distilled
spirits only?
Why: The sample label and class/type example are distilled spirits. Wine and
malt have materially different rules (wine: optional <=14% ABV, metric-only
standards of fill; malt: optional ABV in most cases, U.S. customary net
contents). All three multiply the field-mapping rules.
ANSWER (USER): All three.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.2 EXACT VS FUZZY MATCHING BOUNDARIES
Q: How tolerant should "normalized" comparisons be? At what point does a
normalized difference become Needs Correction vs Rejected?
Why: The regulation is silent on matching; agents apply judgment ("STONE'S
THROW" is "obviously the same"). Over-strict = false failures; over-loose =
missed violations.
Default: normalized compare (case/whitespace/punctuation/unit normalization)
for brand/name/address/class-type; tight fuzzy edit-distance for the warning;
numeric-with-tolerance for ABV. Near a threshold or low OCR confidence ->
Needs Correction (human confirm), not a hard Rejected. Documented judgment call.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.3 WARNING STATEMENT - PUNCTUATION AND LINE BREAKS
Q: The CFR prints the two warning sentences with a line break; real labels may
join or split them. Does the normalizer collapse newlines and whitespace?
Why: Too aggressive masks real deviations; too strict false-fails line-wrapped
labels.
Default: normalize all whitespace (incl. newlines) to single spaces before fuzzy
compare, but do NOT normalize punctuation or case (case of "GOVERNMENT WARNING:"
is itself a requirement).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.4 FORMAT CHECKS (BOLD / TYPE SIZE / CONTRAST) RELIABILITY
Q: Can bold, font size (mm), and contrast be detected reliably enough to be a
deterministic verdict, or are they always heuristic?
Why: These ARE regulatory requirements (16.22, 5.52-5.53, 4.38, 7.53), but
pixel-based detection of bold weight and physical mm is unreliable without a
scale reference.
Default: treat caps (string-level) and presence as deterministic; treat
bold/size/contrast as best-effort heuristic flags with a "human confirm"
indicator. Do not claim these as hard checks in v1. Document the limitation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.5 COUNTRY OF ORIGIN DEPTH
Q: For imports, must the tool implement the substance of CBP's 19 CFR 102/134
marking rules, or only compare the stated origin string?
Why: The assignment lists "country of origin for imports" as a common element,
but the substantive marking rules live OUTSIDE TTB's own CFR parts (TTB defers
to CBP - 5.69, 4.35(e), 7.69).
Default: v1 compares the extracted origin string to the application value
(normalized). Full 102/134 substantive analysis is out of scope and noted.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.6 OCR/VISION MODEL CHOICE AND THE 5-SECOND BUDGET 
Q: Which exact OCR engine and (optional) vision model? Is 5s met at the chosen
image size?
ANSWER (USER): AWS Textract DetectDocumentText (pure/raw text extraction) is the
extractor. CLOSED: AnalyzeDocument (forms/tables), Google Document AI, Azure
Document Intelligence, and self-hosted OCR. Vision-LLM reasoning is reserved
for the Bedrock Boolean criteria (7.6.9), run in parallel.
Why: The 5-second requirement is the hardest constraint and the prior pilot's
cause of death; managed LLM vision can exceed 5s when serialized, so it must
overlap the deterministic checks.
Default: Textract DetectDocumentText on the critical path; Bedrock criteria in
parallel; latency must be MEASURED, not assumed. PaddleOCR on GPU is the
documented data-sovereignty fallback only.
SUPERSEDED: the Bedrock criteria are dropped for the prototype - see 8.17 and
5.5 (deterministic + Textract only).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.7 APPLICATION-DATA SCHEMA

product type (Item 5), source (Item 3), and applicant (Item 8) are the matching
fields; ABV/net-contents/class-type/warning are label-derived.
Remaining caveat: the take-home's "Sample Label" frames ABV/net-contents/

Default: keep the faithful 5100.31 mirror; if "expected values" are wanted, use

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.8 IMAGE SOURCES AND RIGHTS / TEST DATA
Q: Where do test labels come from? The assignment suggests generating synthetic

Why: No real COLA artwork is provided; the prototype must be demonstrable on

Default: generate a small set of synthetic distilled-spirits labels (including

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.9 "FIELD OF VISION" AND PLACEMENT CHECKS
Q: Should the tool enforce placement rules (brand/class/alcohol share one field
of vision for distilled spirits; brand label vs back label)?
Why: Real requirements (5.63(a), 4.32(a)), but "same field of vision" requires
understanding physical container geometry, which a flat photo only approximates.
Default: v1 checks presence and legibility, NOT field-of-vision geometry (defer

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.10 PROOF VS ABV CROSS-CHECK EDGE CASES
Q: When a label states both ABV and proof, is a mismatch always a failure, or
can labels state proof loosely?
Why: Proof = 2 x ABV by definition; 45% = 90 proof is exact, but unusual proofs
(101 proof = 50.5% ABV) and rounding vary.
Default: cross-check proof = 2 x ABV within the ABV tolerance and flag (Needs
Correction), not reject, on inconsistency. The regulation treats proof as
optional supplementary (5.65(b)(1)).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.11 ASPARTAME / INGREDIENT DISCLOSURES

Why: Mandatory when the condition applies (5.63(c), 4.32(c-e), 7.63(b)), but
the application data may not assert them, and "does this product contain X" is
a formula question, not a label question.
Default: v1 checks PRESENCE/format of these statements if the application
asserts them, but does not independently determine whether the condition
applies. Document as a scope boundary.

8.16 PAPER-FORM-AS-IMAGE (EVALUATED AND SHELVED)

8.17 BEDROCK LLM - NOT REQUIRED 
Q: Does the prototype actually need the Bedrock LLM Boolean criteria proposed
in 5.1.2 and 7.6.9, or does deterministic fuzzy-string + Textract suffice?

criteria resolve deterministically (controlled vocabulary for class/type,
position/qualifier heuristics for field mapping, regex synonyms for function
phrases), and the 5s latency budget plus determinism/auditability argue
against a generative criterion.
Resolved: no Bedrock for the prototype - pure determinism + Textract. See 5.5
for the full rationale. Defer a vision model only for future semantic
field-disambiguation needs, behind the deterministic heuristics.
